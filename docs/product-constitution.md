# Product Constitution

Status: normative and stable  
Version: 1.0.0  
Last reviewed: 2026-07-12  
Change threshold: explicit product decision; update the index and affected stage brief

## Mission

The Verifiable Clean Energy Atlas is a public-interest reference for understanding physical low-lifecycle-greenhouse-gas energy infrastructure, the strength of the evidence behind it, and the limits of available coverage.

Its mature mission is to connect that verified physical foundation to accountable financing evidence and, later, to careful public-interest capital guidance. It is not a generic investment marketplace, a promotional list of products labelled green, or a substitute for regulated financial advice.

## Audience order

1. **Journalists first:** facts must be quickly citable and lead directly to definitions, calculations, evidence, and limitations.
2. **Citizens:** the same facts must remain understandable without specialist knowledge.
3. **Energy experts:** observations, methods, conflicts, calculations, versions, and permitted data must be inspectable enough to reproduce or challenge claims.

Journalist-first means evidence is the organizing constraint. It does not permit inaccessible language or concealment of expert detail.

## Product sequence

The product MUST develop cumulatively:

1. **V1 — Verifiable Atlas** establishes physical assets, energy indicators, coverage, methodology, source lineage, and corrections.
2. **V2 — Financing Atlas** attaches source-backed ownership and financing evidence without redefining V1 records.
3. **V3 — Capital Guidance** may add evidence-gated opportunity analysis, accountability, and outcomes without equating sustainability with financial merit.

A later stage MUST satisfy every applicable earlier-stage invariant. Stage labels describe released capability, not marketing ambition.

## Constitutional laws

### Evidence before scale

- Prefer fewer traceable records over impressive counts.
- Never claim worldwide, geographic, or technological completeness without measured evidence.
- The presence of map markers never proves coverage.
- Unknown, conflicting, stale, restricted, partial, and unavailable evidence remains visible.
- No important public claim may be detached from its sources and method.

### Lifecycle truth

- “Clean” is a lifecycle-greenhouse-gas classification, not an operational-emissions label, renewable label, taxonomy badge, or marketing claim.
- Raw-material extraction, manufacturing, construction, fuel or feedstock supply, operation, maintenance, decommissioning, end-of-life, and material indirect effects are considered when applicable.
- Climate classification does not erase biodiversity, land, water, waste, mining, human-rights, governance, safety, or community impacts.
- Renewable generation, renewable heat, recovered heat, nuclear, conditional pathways, carriers, and storage remain distinguishable.

### No invented certainty

- Never fabricate records, geometry, coordinates, capacity, generation, status, ownership, financing, sources, lifecycle evidence, confidence, or coverage.
- Never turn a target date into evidence of completion or operation.
- Never turn a locality, administrative centroid, city center, postal centroid, company office, or nearby country into a facility location.
- Never silently resolve conflicting observations, merge similar entities, or delete history.
- Estimates and analytical allocations remain labelled as estimates and preserve their inputs and uncertainty.

### Separate unlike quantities

- Electrical generation MW, thermal MW, storage MW, and storage MWh MUST NOT be summed into one capacity number.
- Electricity generation share, installed electrical capacity share, total energy supply, and final energy consumption MUST NOT be merged or mislabeled.
- Storage discharge is not primary generation.
- Hydrogen, ammonia, synthetic fuels, batteries, pumped hydro, and other carriers or storage are not primary energy sources.
- Renewable and nuclear totals may be combined only when separate totals are also available and the combined definition is explicit.

### Independence and public trust

- Payment, sponsorship, referral revenue, commissions, partnerships, or provider preference MUST NOT influence inclusion, classification, methodology, verification, coverage status, or ranking.
- Paid placement and sponsored verification are outside the product’s identity.
- Missing evidence never becomes a positive score.
- Sustainability value, additionality, financing need, evidence quality, expected return, and financial risk remain separate dimensions.
- The product MUST NOT promise impact or returns.

### Public auditability

- Methods, formulas, source selection, datasets, releases, changes, corrections, licensing constraints, and limitations are public and versioned to the extent legally permitted.
- Every derived headline value must be traceable through calculation inputs and field observations to original evidence.
- Restricted source data may remain restricted, but the permitted output, method, provenance metadata, restriction, and reproducibility limit must be disclosed.
- Corrections preserve the previous value and use the same validation standards as imports.

### Geographic honesty

- Coverage is evaluated by geography and technology, not inferred from aggregate record count.
- Unreliable locations remain searchable and, when methodologically valid, countable, but unplotted.
- Offshore jurisdiction requires documented territorial, exclusive-economic-zone, high-seas, or disputed-area evidence. Proximity is never legal attribution.
- Cross-country comparisons require compatible definitions, units, and periods.

### Accessibility and durable access

- The map-first experience must remain usable with keyboard and assistive technology and at a 390 px mobile viewport.
- Visual encodings must not rely on color alone; contrast and focus must remain visible.
- Evidence and limitations must not be hidden behind device-specific interactions.
- Shareable state, stable identifiers, open formats where licensing permits, and understandable error states are part of public access.

## Stage boundaries

### V1 includes

Physical facilities and projects, energy and heat indicators, coverage, clean classification, lifecycle evidence, ownership evidence needed for physical records, methodology, provenance, calculation lineage, limitations, corrections, and release history.

### V1 excludes

Financing transactions, funding opportunities, investment or funding-gap rankings, capital recommendations, personalized financial advice, regulated investment access, and money movement. V1 also excludes a general transmission-grid, fossil-fuel, carbon-capture, data-center, or connectivity atlas.

### Future stages

V1 MUST preserve the structural distinctions required for future finance. Those foundations MUST NOT create empty finance panels or delay an otherwise valid V1 release. V2 and V3 activate only under the safeguards in [Future-Financing Compatibility Constraints](future-financing-compatibility.md).

## Product-level success

The product succeeds when:

- a journalist can cite a fact and reach its formula, period, limitations, and original evidence without specialist help;
- a citizen can distinguish verified, partial, conditional, excluded, unknown, and unplotted information;
- an expert can reproduce or challenge published aggregates;
- no geography is called verified without an auditable national baseline;
- no map position or total is invented;
- later financing evidence attaches without redefining facilities, projects, sources, time, or verification;
- public-interest purpose never becomes pay-to-play promotion or a guarantee of impact or return.

## Governance

The constitution changes rarely. Detailed definitions belong in the owning methodology or contract. A policy change that alters the meaning of “clean,” “verified,” a stage boundary, or product independence requires:

1. a written rationale and affected requirements;
2. a versioned methodology or stage update;
3. migration and recalculation of affected published claims;
4. a public change entry and visible impact on coverage or classification;
5. preservation of the previous release for audit.
